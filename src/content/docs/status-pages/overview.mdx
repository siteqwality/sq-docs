A Site Qwality **status page** is the public face of your reliability story. It lives at `https://statuspage.siteqwality.com/<account-id>/<page-id>` (or your custom domain) and shows visitors the current and historical state of the parts of your service you choose to publish, all driven by the same monitors you already have set up internally.

## When to use a status page

- You sell to other businesses and customers ask "where's your status page?" in pre-sales calls.
- You'd rather customers self-serve "is it me or is it down?" instead of opening a support ticket.
- You need a single canonical place to publish incident updates so support doesn't paste the same message into 40 conversations.
- You want a dashboard your VP can refresh during an outage instead of asking you for an ETA in Slack.

## When not to use it

- Don't expose internal dashboards on it. Status pages are for *what's broken from a customer's perspective*. If you publish "DB read replica lag" your customers will wonder what to do with that information.
- Don't make it a marketing surface. Brand it, sure, but the page exists to communicate facts during outages. Heavy custom CSS works against scannability.

## How it works

A status page is composed of three layers:

1. **Status page object**: the container, with branding (logo, colors, theme), a custom domain (optional), and a default theme.
2. **Components**: what the page reports on. Most are links to monitors: an HTTP check or a browser check with a public-facing `friendly_name` and optional `sla_target_percentage`, or a heartbeat monitor. Their status is the monitor's live status. A manual component has no monitor, and you set its status. Any component can carry a status override with a public note.
3. **Incidents and maintenance**: published events. You publish incidents and post their public updates yourself; internal notes never reach the page. A [service](/incident-management/services/overview/) can also publish the incidents it opens automatically, in the words of its public template, and those resolve when their monitors recover. Scheduled maintenance shows future planned downtime.

When a visitor loads the page, the [public status page API](/status-pages/reference/#public-status-page-api) reads the latest status of every component, joins in any open incident or active maintenance, and renders the result via the public Next.js front-end.

<Aside type="tip">
The status page back-end is a separate Lambda (`status-page-api`) reading the same Postgres data as the dashboard, so there's no separate sync step. The page is rendered on request from data that is at most about 30 seconds old, and an open page refreshes its live status every 60 seconds, so a change in the dashboard shows up within about a minute.
</Aside>

## What you can configure

| Concern | Settings |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | `friendly_name`, `page_title`, default theme (`light` / `dark` / `system`) |
| **Branding** | logo and dark theme logo, favicon, OG image, primary/accent color (`#hex`, kept readable in both themes), header & footer text, custom CSS (50KB max), white-label toggle |
| **Custom domain** | bring your own subdomain (e.g. `status.example.com`). Site Qwality provisions an ACM cert and gives you the CNAME to add |
| **Components** | attach any HTTP check, browser check or heartbeat monitor, add manual components, override a public name or status, set an SLA target, group and order them |
| **Incidents** | manually open, publish, update, resolve or unpublish, or have a service publish the incidents it opens automatically |
| **Maintenance** | publish scheduled windows ahead of planned work |
| **Subscribers** | visitors subscribe by email, confirm through a link, and choose incidents, maintenance or both, for every component or only some; you can import a list, and each address still confirms |

<Aside type="caution">
Visitors subscribe with email and confirm the address. We send email on incident create/update and on status-page maintenance, to the subscribers whose choices match. Anyone can also follow incidents by [RSS or Atom](/status-pages/how-to/feeds-badge-embed/). SMS and webhook subscribers are not available.
</Aside>
