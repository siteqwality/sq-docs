A *subscriber* is a visitor to your status page who confirmed an email address. They get an email when you create an incident on the page, post an update to it through the page, or schedule maintenance. SMS, RSS, and webhook subscribers are not available.

<Aside type="caution">
The public status page has an email subscribe form. The visitor confirms the address from a link we send. Until they confirm, they get no mail.
</Aside>

## How it works (today)

The back-end emails every confirmed subscriber on the page in three cases:

- An incident is created on the status page (`POST /status_page/{status_page_id}/incident`).
- An update is posted through the page (`POST /status_page/{status_page_id}/incident/{incident_id}/update`).
- Maintenance is scheduled on the page.

Nothing is sent when you publish an existing incident to a page, post an update through `POST /incident/{incident_id}/update`, resolve an incident, edit maintenance, or when a service publishes an incident automatically. If subscribers should hear about a change, post it as an update through the page.

Visitors subscribe with the form on the public status page, and every email has an unsubscribe link. There is no API for adding or removing subscribers.

A confirmation email is sent on subscribe; the visitor must click the link to activate. This protects against visitors being signed up against their will.

## Best practices

- **Don't auto-subscribe customers.** Always require explicit opt-in. Site Qwality enforces double opt-in via the confirmation email.
- **Send maintenance windows, not flapping checks.** Subscribers expect signal, not noise. Use [maintenance windows](/incident-management/maintenance/overview/) to suppress monitor-driven incidents during planned work; only manually-created incidents should reach subscribers.
- **Keep messages short and actionable.** "We're investigating elevated 500 errors on the API. Affects: checkout. ETA: 30 minutes." is better than three paragraphs of context.
