By the end of this guide you'll have a schedule called `Primary on-call` that hands off every Monday at 09:00 in your timezone, rotating between two engineers. You need at least two users in the account ([invite teammates](/getting-started/account-and-team/#invite-your-team)).

## 1. Create the schedule

<Tabs syncKey="interface">
<TabItem label="Dashboard">

<Steps>

1. Go to **Respond → On-Call** and click **New schedule**.

2. **Name:** `Primary on-call`. **Timezone:** pick yours (defaults to UTC). Click **Create Schedule**.

</Steps>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="API">

With an [API key](/getting-started/account-and-team/#generate-api-keys) exported as `SITEQWALITY_API_KEY`:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/on_call_schedule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "name": "Primary on-call",
    "timezone": "America/Los_Angeles"
  }'
```

Save the returned `id`; you'll attach a layer to it next.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## 2. Add a rotation layer

<Tabs syncKey="interface">
<TabItem label="Dashboard">

<Steps>

1. Open the schedule and click **Add layer** beside **Layers**.

2. Configure:
   - **Rotation Type:** `Weekly`.
   - **Handoff Day:** `Monday`.
   - **Handoff Time:** `09:00`.
   - **Participants:** add Alice and Bob in the order you want them to rotate.

3. Click **Add Layer**.

</Steps>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="API">

`participants` takes user IDs, which `GET /account/info` lists.

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/on_call_schedule/$SCHEDULE_ID/layer \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "rotation_type": "weekly",
    "handoff_day": 1,
    "handoff_time": "09:00",
    "participants": [
      "11111111-aaaa-4bbb-9ccc-dddddddddddd",
      "22222222-aaaa-4bbb-9ccc-eeeeeeeeeeee"
    ]
  }'
```

`handoff_day` is 0=Sunday … 6=Saturday. `handoff_time` is `HH:MM` in 24-hour format, interpreted in the schedule's timezone.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## 3. Confirm who's on call right now

<Tabs syncKey="interface">
<TabItem label="Dashboard">

Open the schedule. Its page shows who is on call now, until when, and who is next, then every shift of the next two weeks with any gap called out.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="API">

```bash
curl https://api.siteqwality.com/on_call_schedule/$SCHEDULE_ID/on_call \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY"
```

The response names who is on call, when the shift started and ends, and the shift after it. Add `?at=2026-11-02T09:30:00Z` to ask about another moment, or call `/on_call_schedule/$SCHEDULE_ID/shifts` for the next two weeks. See the [reference](/incident-management/on-call/reference/#see-whos-on-call).

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## 4. Wire it into an escalation policy

A schedule on its own doesn't page anyone; you need an [escalation policy](/incident-management/escalation/overview/) that targets it.

<Tabs syncKey="interface">
<TabItem label="Dashboard">

<Steps>

1. Go to **Respond → Escalation Policies** and click **New policy**. Name it `Primary on-call escalation` and click **Create Policy**.

2. Open the policy and click **Add level**. Set **Timeout (minutes)** to `5`, pick `Primary on-call` under **Target Schedules**, and click **Add Level**.

</Steps>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="API">

```bash
# Create a policy
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/escalation_policy \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "name": "Primary on-call escalation" }'

# Add a level that pages the schedule
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/escalation_policy/$POLICY_ID/level \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "timeout_minutes": 5,
    "target_schedule_ids": ["'$SCHEDULE_ID'"]
  }'
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

This level pages whoever is on-call in `$SCHEDULE_ID` and waits 5 minutes for them to ack before moving on.
