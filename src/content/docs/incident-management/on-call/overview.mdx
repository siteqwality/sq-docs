An **on-call schedule** is a named rotation of users that, given any timestamp, returns the user who is currently on call. Escalation policies reference schedules instead of hard-coded user lists, so when the rotation hands off, paging follows automatically.

## When to use schedules

- You want pages to follow a rotation rather than always hitting the same person.
- You want handoffs at a fixed local time, kept steady across DST changes.
- You want to handle holiday cover and one-off swaps cleanly.

If you only have one engineer, you don't need a schedule: just point your escalation policy at that user directly.

## How a schedule produces "who's on call"

Each schedule has:

- A **name** (`Primary on-call`, `Eng manager backup`).
- A **timezone**, an IANA name (`America/Los_Angeles`, `Europe/Berlin`, `Asia/Singapore`, `UTC`). Every name in the tz database is accepted; anything else is refused with `400`. Handoff times, handoff days and coverage windows are local times in this zone, so a 09:00 handoff stays at 09:00 local across DST changes.
- One or more **layers**, stacked in priority order. A layer can carry its own timezone, for follow-the-sun.
- Zero or more **overrides**: hard "user X is on from time A to time B" entries.

When asked who's on call at time `T`:

1. Apply overrides first. If any override covers `T`, that user is on call. Done.
2. Otherwise walk layers from the **highest `layer_number` downward**. A layer answers when `T` is inside its `effective_from`/`effective_until` window and inside one of its coverage windows (a layer without windows covers around the clock). The first layer that answers wins; whose turn it is follows from the rotation.

This means **higher layers shadow lower ones while they cover**. Give a higher layer coverage windows (`restrictions`) to make it a night, weekend or regional shift; lower layers answer the rest of the time.

The first participant of a new layer is on call from its `effective_from` (or from when you create it) until the next handoff, then the rotation moves on.

## Layer rotation types

| `rotation_type` | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `daily` | Hand off every day at `handoff_time`. |
| `weekly` | Hand off once a week at `handoff_time`, on `handoff_day` (0=Sunday .. 6=Saturday) when one is set. |
| `custom` | Hand off every `rotation_interval_days` days at `handoff_time`. |

`participants` is an ordered list of user UUIDs; the rotation walks the list and wraps around. A person can appear more than once.

## Coverage windows

`restrictions` limits a layer to recurring windows in its zone:

- **Daily:** `{ "type": "daily", "start_time": "22:00", "duration_minutes": 600 }` covers 22:00 to 08:00 every night. A window can run past midnight.
- **Weekly:** `{ "type": "weekly", "start_day_of_week": 5, "start_time": "18:00", "duration_minutes": 3840 }` covers Friday 18:00 to Monday 10:00 every week.

Windows keep their local hours across DST changes, so the night window above is 22:00 to 08:00 local on the night the clocks change too. Rotation handoffs happen on schedule whether or not the layer covers at that moment.

## Daylight saving time

Handoffs are compared as instants, not as clock readings:

- When the clocks go back, a handoff whose local time happens twice (01:30 in New York on the first Sunday of November) happens once, at the first occurrence. The repeated hour never hands the pager back.
- When the clocks go forward, a handoff whose local time is skipped (02:30 in Berlin on the last Sunday of March) happens at the moment the clocks jump, 03:00 local.

## Overrides

An override beats every layer. Use them for:

- **Holiday cover:** "Bob is covering for Alice from Dec 22 to Jan 2."
- **Swap weekends:** "I'll take next weekend if you take this one."
- **Filling gaps:** if the layers leave a hole, an override patches it.

Overrides are time-bounded with `start_at` and `end_at`, and `end_at` must be after `start_at`. When overrides overlap, the **most recently created one wins**.

## See who's on call

- **Dashboard:** the on-call page shows who is on call now on every schedule. A schedule's page shows who is on call, until when, who is next, and every shift of the next two weeks, gaps included.
- **API:** `GET /on_call_schedule/{id}/on_call` answers for now (or any `at`), `GET /on_call_schedule/{id}/shifts` returns the timeline over a range, and `GET /on_call` lists who is on call across your schedules, or one person's shifts with `user_id`. See the [reference](/incident-management/on-call/reference/#see-whos-on-call).

- **Calendar:** subscribe to your own shifts, or a whole schedule, in Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar. See [In a calendar](/incident-management/on-call/reference/#in-a-calendar).
- **CLI, assistants and Slack:** `siteqwality oncall now`, the MCP tools, and `/oncall` in a connected Slack workspace give the same answer.
- **Notices:** whoever goes on call can be told through their low-urgency notification rules. See [Handoff notices](/incident-management/on-call/reference/#handoff-notices).

Escalation policies use the same answer when they page a schedule.

## Changing a rotation

Edit a layer to change its people, order, handoff time or windows. Whoever is on call stays on until the next handoff, past shifts stay as they were, and the layer keeps its id, so API and Terraform clients keep working. The [on-call report](/incident-management/on-call/reference/#on-call-report) shows who carried how much on-call time, including nights and weekends.

## Common patterns

### Single weekly rotation

One layer, `rotation_type: weekly`, `handoff_day: 1` (Monday), `handoff_time: 09:00`, `participants: [alice, bob, carol]`. Each engineer is on for one week starting Monday morning.

### Business hours with an after-hours rotation

Layer 1: a weekly rotation of the whole team, no windows. Layer 2: a daily rotation of the day crew with `restrictions: [{ "type": "weekly", "start_day_of_week": 1, "start_time": "09:00", "duration_minutes": 480 }, ...]`, one window per weekday. The day crew answers 09:00 to 17:00 on weekdays; layer 1 answers nights and weekends.

### Weekend coverage

A higher layer with one weekly window, `start_day_of_week: 5`, `start_time: "18:00"`, `duration_minutes: 3840` (Friday 18:00 to Monday 10:00), and the weekend volunteers as participants.

### Follow the sun

Three layers, each with its own `timezone` and one daily 8-hour window in local time: an APAC layer in `Australia/Sydney` from 09:00, an EMEA layer in `Europe/London` from 09:00, and an Americas layer in `America/New_York` from 09:00. Add a bottom layer without windows to catch any hour the three leave uncovered.

### Primary + secondary

Two separate schedules: `Primary` and `Secondary`. Escalation policy: level 1 → `Primary`, level 2 → `Secondary`. Each schedule rotates independently.

## Pricing

The free plan includes 1 on-call responder. Rotations need more than one person, so they need a responder pack. See [pricing](https://siteqwality.com/pricing/).

A responder is a distinct person in the account who is in a rotation layer that has not ended, or named directly on an escalation level. Someone in three rotations and two levels is one responder. Overrides do not count, and neither does a schedule named on a level, since its people already count through its layers. The limit is checked when you add a layer or a level. Removing someone from the account frees their place.
