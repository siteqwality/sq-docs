An **escalation policy** is the rule for *what to do if no one acknowledges an incident*. It's an ordered list of levels; each level fires its targets, waits its timeout, and if no acknowledgment arrives, the next level fires.

## What starts an escalation

An escalation runs when an incident opens on a [service](/incident-management/services/overview/) that has an escalation policy, whether a monitor opened it or you [created it by hand](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/). The policy is attached to the service, not to the monitor or the notification group, so an incident with no service, or whose service has no policy, never escalates.

## Anatomy

Each level has a `timeout_minutes` and its targets: users (`target_user_ids`), on-call schedules (`target_schedule_ids`), or both, as in "page Alice *and* whoever's on the platform schedule, wait 5 minutes, then move on." Levels are numbered `1` to `N` and run in that order; see [levels are positions](/incident-management/escalation/reference/#levels-are-positions) for how adding, deleting and reordering renumber them.

On the policy, [`repeat_count`](#repeat-cycles) sets how many more passes to run after the first, and `on_exhausted` sets what happens when nobody acknowledges: `notify_owners` (default), `stop` or `escalate_to_policy`. Every field is in the [reference](/incident-management/escalation/reference/).

## Lifecycle

```
Incident opens
     │
     ▼
┌─ Level 1 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [on_call_primary] │
│ timeout: 5 min             │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 5 min
         │
         ▼
┌─ Level 2 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [on_call_backup]  │
│ timeout: 10 min            │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 10 min
         │
         ▼
┌─ Level 3 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [eng_manager]     │
│ timeout: 15 min            │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 15 min
         │
         ▼
   repeat (per repeat_count), then on_exhausted
```

When a level starts, it pages each target through that person's own [notification rules](/incident-management/notifications/overview/#notification-rules-per-user). On-call schedules resolve at that moment, so handoffs work transparently. The timeout counts from when the level starts, so time spent paging does not lengthen it.

A rule whose contact method cannot page (an unverified SMS number, a bounced email address, no SMS left on the account) is skipped. A user no rule can reach is paged at their [sign-in email](/incident-management/escalation/readiness/#the-sign-in-email-fallback) instead. Both are recorded on the incident.

## A level that reaches nobody is skipped

When a level starts and nothing would go out to any of its targets (no targets, nobody on call, every target left the account, or every address bounced including the sign-in email), it is skipped at once instead of waiting out its timeout, and the next level starts. The incident records an `escalation_unreachable` event naming the level and why each target was out of reach. The [readiness check](/incident-management/escalation/readiness/) uses the same definition.

A level that reaches anyone at all, through a working rule, a rule delayed past the timeout or the sign-in email fallback, is not skipped. If a whole round of levels reaches nobody, or the policy has no levels, the policy runs out at once and [`on_exhausted`](#when-nobody-acknowledges) applies.

## What a page says

Every page names the incident, its service and the monitors in it, and why this person is being paged: the level and pass, the policy, and the on-call schedule that put them there. For example, an email reads "You are paged by level 2 of 3 of the escalation policy "Platform", pass 2 of 3, as on call for Primary on-call." SMS carries a short form such as `L2/3 via Primary on-call`, and Slack adds the line under the incident fields.

## When nobody acknowledges

A policy runs out when its last level times out on the last pass, or as soon as no level reaches anyone. What happens then is the policy's `on_exhausted`:

| `on_exhausted` | What happens |
|---|---|
| `notify_owners` (default) | Every owner and admin of the account gets one email saying the policy ran out and nobody owns the incident. |
| `stop` | Nothing is sent. |
| `escalate_to_policy` | The policy named in `escalate_to_policy_id` runs once, from its first level. When it runs out too, the owners and admins get the email, whatever that policy's own `on_exhausted` says. |

In every case the incident records an `escalation_exhausted` event, and the dashboard shows a banner on the incident until someone acknowledges it.

`escalate_to_policy` follows one hop only: the fallback never hands over again, so two policies that fall back to each other cannot loop. Reopening the incident starts over on the service's own policy. A policy that another policy falls back to cannot be deleted until you give those policies another fallback.

## Acknowledging stops it

Acknowledging or resolving the incident cancels the escalation: neither its pending timeout nor any per-user delayed page will fire. Every route into acknowledgment does this:

- `POST /incident/{id}/acknowledge` and `POST /incident/{id}/resolve`
- the [acknowledge and resolve links](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/one-click-actions/) in a paged responder's email and SMS
- the dashboard's incident page
- [acknowledging the monitor outage](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/acknowledge-outages/#incidents-opened-by-the-outage) that opened the incident
- automatic recovery, when the monitor that opened the incident comes back

Running out is different. An `exhausted` policy stops only the level cycling, so a per-user rule's delayed page (`delay_minutes`) that was already scheduled still fires while the incident is unacknowledged.

## When an incident changes while it escalates

- **Severity goes up.** Raising an incident from `minor` to `major` or `critical` pages the current level again, and an acknowledged incident goes back to `triggered`.
- **It moves to another service.** The old service's escalation is canceled and the new service's policy starts at level one, but only while the incident is `triggered`. See [move an incident to another service](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/#move-an-incident-to-another-service).
- **You add participants.** Adding a user, a schedule or a policy as [participants](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/participants/) pages each person through their own notification rules.

## Targets vs notification groups

- **Notification groups** are the raw fanout: "this monitor's alerts go to these channels." Channel-level. No escalation.
- **Escalation policies** are the routing for incidents: "if no one acks, move on." User and schedule level, hooking into per-user notification rules.

A monitor's alert always goes to its notification groups, and nothing in a group starts an escalation. A common setup: the group sends a Slack message for visibility, and the service's escalation policy pages the on-call.

## Repeat cycles

`repeat_count` is **how many more times the policy runs through its levels after the first pass**, from `0` to `20`, default `0`. When the last level times out, the policy goes back to level 1 for another pass. When the repeats are used up, the escalation becomes `exhausted` and [`on_exhausted`](#when-nobody-acknowledges) applies.

| `repeat_count` | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `0` (default) | One pass through every level, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `1` | Two passes, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `3` | Four passes, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `20` | The maximum: 21 passes. |

## When to use multiple policies

- One per **service** if your on-call rotations differ by service ownership.
- One per **environment** if `prod` should hit on-call but `staging` should only ping a Slack channel.
