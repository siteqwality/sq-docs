An **escalation policy** is the rule for *what to do if no one acknowledges an incident*. It's an ordered list of levels; each level fires its targets, waits its timeout, and if no acknowledgement arrives, the next level fires.

## What starts an escalation

An escalation runs when an incident that has a `service_id` opens, and that [service](/incident-management/services/overview/) has an `escalation_policy_id` set. That is the only wiring: the policy is attached to the service, not to the monitor and not to the notification group.

This applies equally to incidents a monitor opened automatically and to incidents you [created by hand](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/). An incident with no `service_id`, or whose service has no policy, never escalates.

<Aside type="note" title="Levels are positions">
Levels are always numbered `1` to `N`. Adding a level in the middle moves the ones below it down, deleting one moves the ones below it up, and `PUT /escalation_policy/{policy_id}/level/reorder` rewrites the order in one go. An open incident follows its level through a reorder. See [levels are positions](/incident-management/escalation/reference/#levels-are-positions).
</Aside>

## Anatomy

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `name` | Human label. `"Primary on-call escalation"`. |
| `repeat_count` | How many more passes through the levels to run after the first. `0` to `20`, default `0`: every level is paged once. |
| `on_exhausted` | What happens when nobody acknowledges: `notify_owners` (default), `stop` or `escalate_to_policy`. See [when nobody acknowledges](#when-nobody-acknowledges). |
| `escalate_to_policy_id` | The policy `escalate_to_policy` runs once. Null otherwise. |
| `levels[]` | Ordered list of `EscalationLevel`s. |

Each level:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `level_number` | Position, from `1`. Added last if omitted. |
| `timeout_minutes` | How long to wait for an ack before moving on. |
| `target_user_ids` | Page these specific users (their notification rules + contact methods kick in). |
| `target_schedule_ids` | Page whoever's on-call in these schedules right now. |

Both target arrays can be combined on a single level: "page Alice *and* whoever's on the platform schedule, wait 5 minutes, then move on."

## Lifecycle

```
Incident opens
     │
     ▼
┌─ Level 1 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [on_call_primary] │
│ timeout: 5 min             │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 5 min
         │
         ▼
┌─ Level 2 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [on_call_backup]  │
│ timeout: 10 min            │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 10 min
         │
         ▼
┌─ Level 3 ──────────────────┐
│ targets: [eng_manager]     │
│ timeout: 15 min            │
└────────┬───────────────────┘
         │
   no ack within 15 min
         │
         ▼
   repeat (per repeat_count), then on_exhausted
```

Each level does three things in order: start a one-shot timeout for `timeout_minutes`, resolve its targets (on-call schedules resolve at firing time, so handoffs work transparently), and page each resolved user through their own [notification rules](/incident-management/notifications/overview/#notification-rules-per-user). The timeout counts from when the level starts, so time spent paging does not lengthen it, and the policy advances within a few seconds of it running out.

A rule whose contact method cannot page (an unverified SMS number, a bounced email address, no SMS left on the account) is skipped. A user no rule can reach is paged at their sign-in email instead. Both are recorded on the incident.

## A level that reaches nobody is skipped

When a level starts, its targets are judged with the same definition the [readiness check](/incident-management/escalation/readiness/) uses. If nothing would go out to any of them (no targets, nobody on call, every target left the account, or every address bounced including the sign-in email), the level is skipped at once instead of waiting out its timeout, and the incident records an `escalation_unreachable` event naming the level and why each target was out of reach. The next level starts straight away.

A level counts as reaching someone when at least one target gets anything: a working rule, a rule delayed past the timeout, or the sign-in email fallback. Only a level that reaches nobody at all is skipped.

If a whole round of levels reaches nobody, the policy runs out at once and [`on_exhausted`](#when-nobody-acknowledges) applies, so a policy that can reach no one never pages nobody in silence. A policy with no levels runs out the same way.

## What a page says

Every page names the incident, its service and the monitors in it, and why this person is being paged: the level and pass, the policy, and the on-call schedule that put them there. For example, an email reads "You are paged by level 2 of 3 of the escalation policy "Platform", pass 2 of 3, as on call for Primary on-call." SMS carries a short form such as `L2/3 via Primary on-call`, and Slack adds the line under the incident fields.

## When nobody acknowledges

A policy runs out when its last level times out on the last pass, or as soon as no level reaches anyone. What happens then is the policy's `on_exhausted`:

| `on_exhausted` | What happens |
|---|---|
| `notify_owners` (default) | Every owner and admin of the account gets one email saying the policy ran out and nobody owns the incident. |
| `stop` | Nothing is sent. |
| `escalate_to_policy` | The policy named in `escalate_to_policy_id` runs once, from its first level. When it runs out too, the owners and admins get the email, whatever that policy's own `on_exhausted` says. |

In every case the escalation becomes `exhausted`, the incident records an `escalation_exhausted` event with the reason (`repeats_used`, `nobody_reachable` or `no_levels`) and who was emailed, and the incident's `unassigned_after_exhaustion` flag turns on, which the dashboard shows as a banner on the incident until someone acknowledges it. An owner whose address bounced is recorded with a `page_skipped` event instead.

`escalate_to_policy` follows one hop only. The fallback never hands over again, so two policies that fall back to each other cannot loop, and a policy cannot name itself (`400`). A handover records an `escalation_policy_handoff` event. Reopening the incident starts over on the service's own policy.

A policy that another policy falls back to cannot be deleted: the API returns `409` listing those policies. Give them another fallback first.

## Acknowledging stops it

Acknowledging or resolving the incident cancels the escalation: its state moves to `acknowledged`, and neither its pending timeout nor any per-user delayed page will fire. The incident itself keeps existing, and the responder works the fix.

Every route into acknowledgement does this, so it does not matter how the responder answers:

- `POST /incident/{id}/acknowledge` and `POST /incident/{id}/resolve`
- the [acknowledge and resolve links](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/one-click-actions/) in a paged responder's email and SMS
- the dashboard's incident page
- [acknowledging the monitor outage](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/acknowledge-outages/#incidents-opened-by-the-outage) that opened the incident
- automatic recovery, when the monitor that opened the incident comes back

<Aside type="caution" title="An exhausted policy can still page once more">
`exhausted` only stops the **level cycling**. If a responder had a per-user rule with a `delay_minutes` that was already scheduled, that delayed page still fires while the incident remains unacknowledged.

Acknowledging or resolving is the only thing that suppresses those. Waiting for the policy to exhaust is not.
</Aside>

## When an incident changes while it escalates

- **Severity goes up.** Raising an incident from `minor` to `major` or `critical` pages the current level again, and an acknowledged incident goes back to `triggered`.
- **It moves to another service.** The old service's escalation is cancelled and the new service's policy starts at level one, but only while the incident is `triggered`. See [move an incident to another service](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/#move-an-incident-to-another-service).
- **You add participants.** Adding a user, a schedule or a policy as [participants](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/participants/) pages each person through their own notification rules.

## Targets vs notification groups

Two different mechanisms with overlapping vibes:

- **Notification groups** are the raw fanout: "this monitor's alerts go to these channels." Channel-level. No escalation.
- **Escalation policies** are the routing for incidents: "if no one acks, move on." User and schedule level, hooking into per-user notification rules.

The two paths are separate. A monitor's alert always goes to its notification groups, and nothing in a group starts an escalation. An escalation starts only when an incident opens on a service with a policy. In practice: the group sends a Slack message for visibility, and the service's escalation policy is what pages the on-call.

## Repeat cycles

`repeat_count` is **how many more times the policy runs through its levels after the first pass**. It accepts `0` to `20` and defaults to `0`, in the API and in the dashboard alike.

Walking the last level's timeout takes the policy back to level 1 for another pass. When the repeats are used up, the escalation becomes `exhausted` and [`on_exhausted`](#when-nobody-acknowledges) applies.

| `repeat_count` | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `0` (default) | One pass through every level, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `1` | Two passes, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `3` | Four passes, then `on_exhausted`. |
| `20` | The maximum: 21 passes. |

Policies created through the API before 2026-10-20 without a `repeat_count` got `3`; they keep it.

## Escalation state

Each incident carries one escalation state. The incident API does not return it, but an incident's detail response has `unassigned_after_exhaustion`, true while the state is `exhausted` and nobody has acknowledged.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `active` | Cycling through levels. |
| `acknowledged` | Someone acked or resolved. Paging stopped, pending schedules deleted. |
| `exhausted` | The policy ran out without an acknowledgement and `on_exhausted` has applied. Level cycling has stopped. |

## When to use multiple policies

- One per **service** if your on-call rotations differ by service ownership.
- One per **environment** if `prod` should hit on-call but `staging` should only ping a Slack channel.
