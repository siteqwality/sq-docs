Notification rules are how each user tells Site Qwality "for high-urgency pages, reach me this way; for low-urgency, this way." They are personal, and they apply to incident pages only: an escalation policy pages you, and your rules decide which [contact methods](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/contact-methods/) fire and when. Monitor alerts go to notification groups and never read these rules (see [who gets notified](/incident-management/notifications/default-routing/)).

The mental model: **stack rules at increasing `delay_minutes` for personal escalation**.

## What you start with

A new user (sign-up, accepted invite or SCIM) starts with their sign-in email as a verified contact method and two rules on it: one `high` and one `low`, both with `delay_minutes: 0`. So they are paged by email at both urgencies until they change it.

When you add a contact method and have no rule for an urgency (for example, you deleted your `low` rule), Site Qwality creates that rule on the new method with no delay. For an SMS number this happens when you verify it.

In the dashboard, rules are under your personal settings: open the menu under your name (bottom left), choose **Notifications**, then **Paging rules**.

## How a rule works

A rule has an `urgency`, a `contact_method_id`, a `delay_minutes` and a `position`.

- **`urgency`** is `high` or `low`. You do not choose it per incident: `critical` and `major` incidents page at high urgency, `minor` at low.
- **`delay_minutes`** is how long after you are paged the rule fires, `0` to `1440`, default `0`. When a delayed rule fires, Site Qwality first checks that the incident is still triggered, nobody has acknowledged it, and the rule still exists; otherwise it does not send.
- **`position`** is sort order within the urgency. It does not stagger anything: every matching rule fires in the same pass, each after its own delay.

A user with no rule for the incident's urgency is not paged.

<Aside type="caution" title="Verify SMS numbers first">
A rule cannot use an SMS number until it is verified. Creating or updating a rule with an unverified SMS method returns `409` with code `contact_method_unverified`: "Verify this phone number before using it in a notification rule." See [verify a contact method](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/contact-methods/#verify-a-contact-method). Email and Slack DM methods can be used straight away.
</Aside>

List your contact methods to get their ids:

```bash
curl https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/contact_method \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY"
```

## Recommended high-urgency stack

Three rules at increasing delays: SMS at 0 minutes, Slack DM at 5, email at 15. Your starting `high` rule on your sign-in email is the third one; move it to 15 minutes instead of adding another.

Verify the SMS number first, then:

```bash
# 0 min: SMS me
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "urgency": "high",
    "contact_method_id": "<sms-id>",
    "delay_minutes": 0,
    "position": 0
  }'

# 5 min: Slack DM me
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "urgency": "high",
    "contact_method_id": "<slack-dm-id>",
    "delay_minutes": 5,
    "position": 1
  }'

# 15 min: email me (update the starting high rule)
curl -X PUT https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule/<email-high-rule-id> \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "delay_minutes": 15, "position": 2 }'
```

`position` is required on create. On update, every field is optional and only the fields you send change. List your rules with `GET /user/$USER_ID/notification_rule` to find their ids.

## Recommended low-urgency stack

For low urgency, one delivery to a low-impact method is enough. Your starting `low` rule (email, no delay) already does that.

To stop low-urgency pages entirely, delete the rule. Without a `low` rule you are skipped for low-urgency pages.

```bash
curl -X DELETE https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule/<email-low-rule-id> \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY"
```

## How the stack interacts with escalation

Escalation policy timeouts and personal-rule delays are **independent timers**. If your escalation policy fires a level at minute 0 and you have rules at 0/5/15 min:

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 min | Escalation level fires you. Your rule at 0 min fires (SMS). |
| 5 min | Your rule at 5 min fires (Slack DM). Still no ack. |
| 5 min | Escalation policy advances to level 2 and pages the next person. |
| 15 min | Your rule at 15 min fires (email). |

You'll keep getting your full personal stack even after escalation has moved on. Acknowledging the incident at any point stops everything.

## Tips

- **Don't put SMS at high delay.** SMS is the fastest signal; reserve it for `delay_minutes: 0`.
- **Pair SMS with a second method.** An SMS is tried once, so a later Slack DM or email catches a message that did not arrive.
- **Email is the catcher's mitt.** A 15-minute email rule means "if I missed everything, at least the message is in my inbox."

<Aside type="tip">
A `slack_dm` contact method is quieter than SMS but more visible than email, a good middle option for "I want to know but don't need to wake up."
</Aside>
