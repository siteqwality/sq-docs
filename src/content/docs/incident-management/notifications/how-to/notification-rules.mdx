Notification rules are personal, and they apply to incident pages only: an escalation policy pages you, and your rules decide which [contact methods](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/contact-methods/) fire and when. Monitor alerts go to notification groups and never read these rules (see [who gets notified](/incident-management/notifications/default-routing/)).

## What you start with

A new user (sign-up, accepted invite or SCIM) starts with their sign-in email as a verified contact method and two rules on it: one `high` and one `low`, both with `delay_minutes: 0`. So they are paged by email at both urgencies until they change it.

Adding a contact method when you have no rule for an urgency creates one for you (see [rules created for you](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/contact-methods/#rules-created-for-you)).

In the dashboard, rules are under your personal settings: open the menu under your name (bottom left), choose **Notifications**, then **Paging rules**. Each rule shows as a step in the **High urgency** or **Low urgency** column, in delay order.

## How a rule works

A rule has an `urgency`, a `contact_method_id`, a `delay_minutes` and a `position`.

- **`urgency`** is `high` or `low`. You do not choose it per incident: `critical` and `major` incidents page at high urgency, `minor` at low.
- **`delay_minutes`** is how long after you are paged the rule fires, `0` to `1440`, default `0`. When a delayed rule fires, Site Qwality first checks that the incident is still triggered, nobody has acknowledged it, and the rule still exists; otherwise it does not send.
- **`position`** is sort order within the urgency. It does not stagger anything: every matching rule fires in the same pass, each after its own delay.

A user with no rule for the incident's urgency, or whose rules all fail (an unverified SMS number, an email address that bounced), is paged at their sign-in email instead. The incident records an `escalation_fallback_email` event. See [the sign-in email fallback](/incident-management/escalation/readiness/#the-sign-in-email-fallback).

## Recommended high-urgency stack

Three rules at increasing delays: SMS at 0 minutes, Slack DM at 5, email at 15. SMS delivery is best-effort, so the later rules catch a message that did not arrive. Your starting `high` rule on your sign-in email is the third one; move it to 15 minutes instead of adding another.

Add the SMS number and the Slack DM first. A rule cannot use an SMS number until it is [verified](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/contact-methods/#verify-a-contact-method); email and Slack DM methods can be used straight away. Then, under **Paging rules**:

<Steps>

1. Under **High urgency**, click **Add step**. Under **Reach me by**, pick the SMS number; under **Send it**, pick **Immediately**. Click **Add step**.

2. Add a second step the same way: the Slack DM, at **5 min**.

3. Click the pencil icon on your starting email step, pick **15 min** and click **Save changes**.

</Steps>

**Send it** offers **Immediately**, 5, 10, 15 and 30 minutes and 1 hour, or a custom delay of up to 1440 minutes.

Through the API, with your user ID from `GET /account/info` as `USER_ID`, list your contact methods to get their ids:

```bash
curl https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/contact_method \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY"
```

Then create the two rules and move the email one:

```bash
# 0 min: SMS me
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "urgency": "high",
    "contact_method_id": "<sms-id>",
    "delay_minutes": 0,
    "position": 0
  }'

# 5 min: Slack DM me
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "urgency": "high",
    "contact_method_id": "<slack-dm-id>",
    "delay_minutes": 5,
    "position": 1
  }'

# 15 min: email me (update the starting high rule)
curl -X PUT https://api.siteqwality.com/user/$USER_ID/notification_rule/<email-high-rule-id> \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "delay_minutes": 15, "position": 2 }'
```

`position` is required on create. On update, every field is optional and only the fields you send change. List your rules with `GET /user/$USER_ID/notification_rule` to find their ids.

## Recommended low-urgency stack

For low urgency, one delivery to a low-impact method is enough. Your starting `low` rule (email, no delay) already does that.

Deleting your `low` rule does not stop low-urgency pages: with no rule, they go to your sign-in email. To keep them out of the way, point the `low` rule at an email address or Slack DM you check less often.

## How the stack interacts with escalation

Escalation policy timeouts and personal-rule delays are **independent timers**. If an escalation level with a 5-minute timeout pages you at minute 0 and you have rules at 0/5/15 min:

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 min | Escalation level fires you. Your rule at 0 min fires (SMS). |
| 5 min | Your rule at 5 min fires (Slack DM). Still no ack. |
| 5 min | Escalation policy advances to level 2 and pages the next person. |
| 15 min | Your rule at 15 min fires (email). |

You'll keep getting your full personal stack even after escalation has moved on. Acknowledging the incident at any point stops everything.
