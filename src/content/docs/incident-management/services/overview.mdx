A **service** is a named part of your product, such as `Checkout API` or `Marketing site`. It ties monitors to incident handling:

- which monitors belong to it;
- whether a failing monitor opens an incident on its own;
- which [escalation policy](/incident-management/escalation/overview/) pages people for its incidents;
- the severity those incidents start with;
- who else is told about its incidents (broadcast targets).

Services are the only way a monitor failure becomes an incident, and the only way an incident pages anyone. A monitor with no service still sends its monitor alerts through its [notification groups](/incident-management/notifications/overview/#notification-groups), but it never opens an incident.

## Create a service

<Steps>

1. Open **Manage → Services** and choose **Add service**.

2. Give it a name and, optionally, a description.

3. Pick an **escalation policy**. Without one, the service's incidents page nobody.

4. Pick an **incident creation mode** (see below) and a **default severity**.

5. Set the [reopen window](#reopen-window) and [auto-resolve](#auto-resolve).

6. Choose the **HTTP monitors**, **cron monitors** (heartbeats) and **browser checks** that belong to the service, and save.

</Steps>

Or via the API:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/service \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "name": "Checkout API",
    "escalation_policy_id": "<policy-id>",
    "incident_creation_mode": "auto",
    "reopen_window_minutes": 30,
    "auto_resolve": "unless_acknowledged",
    "default_incident_severity": "major"
  }'
```

Then [link its monitors](#link-monitors).

Scoped API keys, M2M API clients and AI assistants need `read:services` to read services and `write:services` to change them. An AI assistant connected to [MCP](/integrations/mcp/#services) with an API key can list, create and update services, and link or unlink their monitors.

## Service health

The **Services** page and each service's own page show its health. Health is the worse of its incidents and its monitors:

| Health | When |
|---|---|
| Major outage | An open critical incident, or every active monitor of the service failing. |
| Partial outage | An open major incident, or some monitors failing. |
| Degraded | An open minor incident. |
| Operational | No open incident and no failing monitor. |

Paused monitors and merged incidents do not count. A running [maintenance window](/incident-management/maintenance/overview/) that names the service shows a **Maintenance** badge beside the health instead of changing it, because maintenance holds paging, never the status: a forgotten window cannot hide a real outage. Only a window whose end is still in the future counts.

Beside the health, each service shows its open incidents, failing monitors, when its last incident opened and **who is on call now**: the person on the service's own on-call schedule if you set one, otherwise everyone on the first level of its escalation policy (people named directly, and whoever each schedule on that level has on call).

The service page also shows the last 30 days: incidents opened, mean time to acknowledge, mean time to resolve, and uptime, the share of that time with no open incident on the service.

Set a service's team, tags and on-call schedule in **Edit Settings** on its page.

## Incident creation modes

| Mode | What a failing linked monitor does |
|---|---|
| `manual` (default) | Nothing. You open incidents yourself; one you attach to the service still pages its policy and tells its broadcast targets. |
| `auto` | Opens the service's auto-created incident, or joins it if one is already open. See [auto-created incidents](#auto-created-incidents). |

HTTP monitors open incidents only after their own confirmation settings pass, and heartbeats and browser checks are always treated as confirmed.

## Auto-created incidents

A service in `auto` mode has **at most one open auto-created incident**. The first linked monitor to fail opens it, titled `<monitor name> is down`. While it is open, every other failing monitor of the service joins it instead of opening a second one. Each join is recorded on the timeline; see [affected monitors and impact](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/affected-monitors/).

The incident starts at the service's default severity and pages the service's escalation policy as soon as it is recorded. While a [maintenance window](/incident-management/maintenance/overview/) covers the monitor, by its tags or by its service, the incident waits: it opens when the window ends if the monitor is still failing, and never opens if the monitor recovers first. A service switched off with `"disabled": true` opens no automatic incidents until you switch it back on.

Every linked monitor type counts, and the incident counts as recovered when every one of its monitors is healthy again. What healthy means depends on the type:

| Monitor type | Fails when | Recovers when |
|---|---|---|
| HTTP, heartbeat, browser check | Its check goes down. | Its check is up again. |
| TLS certificate | The certificate has expired. A check that cannot reach the site keeps the last known expiry date, so a blip neither opens nor resolves anything. | A valid certificate is read. |
| Domain registration | The registration has expired, on the same rule as TLS. | The registry reports a later expiry date. |
| [DNS records](/uptime-monitoring/dns-record-monitors/overview/) | A change alert is sent, or the zone becomes unreadable (after two failed checks in a row). | A check matches the baseline again, for example after you accept the change. |
| Page speed | The performance score drops below the job's threshold. | The score is back at or above the threshold. |

### Reopen window

When an auto-created incident has resolved, a monitor that fails again within `reopen_window_minutes` of the incident's last alert reopens that incident instead of opening a new one. A later failure opens a new incident.

The window runs from `0` to `1440` minutes. New services start at `30`. `0` never reopens: every failure after a resolve opens a new incident.

### Auto-resolve

When every monitor on the incident has recovered, `auto_resolve` decides whether it resolves itself and stops the escalation:

| Value | What happens |
|---|---|
| `always` | The incident resolves itself. |
| `unless_acknowledged` (default) | It resolves itself unless someone acknowledged it, including by [acknowledging the outage](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/acknowledge-outages/#incidents-opened-by-the-outage) that opened it. An acknowledged incident stays open for a person to resolve. |
| `never` | It stays open until someone resolves it. |

## Link monitors

Every type below can join a service with `POST /service/{id}/monitors`, one or more at a time, and leave it with `DELETE /service/{id}/monitors/{type}/{monitor_id}`. Neither touches the service's other monitors.

| Monitor type | `monitor_type` | Other ways to link it |
|---|---|---|
| HTTP monitors | `http` | **Service** when creating the monitor, the service form, or `service_id` on `POST /http/job` and `PUT /http/job/{id}`. |
| Heartbeat (cron) monitors | `cron` | The service form, or `service_id` on `POST /cron/job` and `PUT /cron/job/{id}`. |
| Browser checks | `browser` | The service form, or `service_id` on `POST /browser_check` and `PUT /browser_check/{id}`. |
| DNS record monitors | `dns_record` | `service_id` on `POST /dns/record-monitor` and `PUT /dns/record-monitor/{id}`. |
| TLS certificate, domain registration and page speed checks of an HTTP monitor | `tls`, `dns`, `page_speed` | Only the link endpoint. |
| Alert rules | | In the rule's own settings. |
| RUM | | Cannot be linked. |

A monitor belongs to at most one service. To link one that belongs to another service, send `"move": true`; the dashboard's service form moves it. The HTTP monitor edit form shows its service read-only, so change it from the service form.

## Broadcast targets

A broadcast target tells someone outside the escalation chain about the service's incidents. There are three types:

- **Email:** any address. An address outside your members' email domains needs a confirmation.
- **Slack channel:** a channel of a connected [Slack integration](/integrations/slack/), or a bot token you paste in. With the integration, nothing secret is stored on the target. A pasted token is write-only and is never shown again.
- **Webhook:** a connected [webhook integration](/integrations/webhooks/), or a public URL you paste in. Deliveries use the [webhook envelope](/integrations/webhooks/#incident-webhooks), with one `incident.*` event per lifecycle event, and are signed. A webhook integration signs with its own secret and shows the deliveries in its log; a pasted URL gets a signing secret of its own, shown once when you add it.

Each target can take only some incidents: a minimum severity, compared with the highest severity the incident reaches, so a target that hears an escalation also hears how it ends, and a list of the lifecycle events it wants.

The Slack, email and webhook targets deliver every lifecycle event: **opened**, **acknowledged**, **updated**, **resolved**, **reopened**, **merged** and **service changed**. Each message names the incident, its service, severity and status, says what happened in one line, and links to the incident in Site Qwality. A broadcast never carries an acknowledge or resolve link, because a channel or shared inbox is not one person: act on the incident in the dashboard.

| Event | `events` name | What the message says | Webhook `event` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opened | `incident_opened` | The incident and its severity | `incident.created` |
| Acknowledged | `incident_acknowledged` | Acknowledged by Ana Diaz | `incident.acknowledged` |
| Update posted | `incident_updated` | Update posted by Ana Diaz, then the update's text | `incident.updated` |
| Severity changed | `incident_updated` | Severity changed from minor to major | `incident.updated` |
| Title changed | `incident_updated` | Title changed (the message shows the new title) | `incident.updated` |
| Resolved | `incident_resolved` | The incident, with status resolved | `incident.resolved` |
| Reopened | `incident_reopened` | Reopened, then the reopen update's text if there is one | `incident.reopened` |
| Merged | `incident_merged` | Merged into Payments API errors | `incident.merged` |
| Service changed | `incident_service_changed` | Moved from service Checkout to service Payments | `incident.service_changed` |

An internal update is announced without its text: the message reads "Internal update posted by Ana Diaz (open the incident to read it)". Only public update text leaves Site Qwality. A service change goes to the targets of both the old and the new service.

For example, an acknowledgement reaches an email target with the subject `[Checkout] Acknowledged by Ana Diaz: checkout-api is down`, and a Slack channel as:

```text
:large_orange_circle: MAJOR Incident: checkout-api is down
Acknowledged by Ana Diaz
Service: Checkout          Severity: major
Status: acknowledged       Monitors: None
Open the incident in Site Qwality to acknowledge or resolve it.
```

Opened and resolved messages look as they always have.

In the dashboard, open the service from **Manage → Services** and click **+ Add Target** under **Notification Targets**. Through the API, see the [reference](/incident-management/services/reference/#broadcast-targets).

## Manual incidents

An incident you open by hand on a service starts at the service's default severity unless you pick one. It is never published by itself: publish it from the incident, where the service's public template prefills the words.

## Publish incidents to a status page

**Auto-publish to Status Page** (`auto_publish_to_status_page_id`) puts every incident the service opens automatically on that [status page](/status-pages/overview/) and emails the page's [subscribers](/status-pages/how-to/subscribers/) that it opened.

The **public incident template** (`public_incident_template`) words what customers read, so a monitor's name or an internal hostname never reaches the page:

- **Public incident title**: the title the page shows. Without one, an automatic incident shows "Service disruption".
- **First public update**: posted when the incident is published. Without one: "We are investigating this issue."
- **Resolved update**: posted when it resolves. Without one: "This incident has been resolved."

Set both on the service form.

## Deleting a service

Only admins, or an API key with `write:services`, can delete a service. Deleting it removes its broadcast targets, detaches its incidents and unlinks its monitors, which keep running.

## Limits

An account can have up to 100 services. Ask support if you need more.
