An outage can open more than one incident: a teammate files one by hand while a monitor opens another, or two services each open their own. Merging folds the duplicates (the **sources**) into the incident you keep (the **target**), so responders and customers follow one timeline. Pick as the target the incident you want people to follow, usually the one that already has the right service, status-page entry and lead.

## What a merge does

- **Sources resolve as merged.** Each source becomes `resolved`, marked as merged into the target. A merged incident cannot be reopened.
- **Monitors and participants move.** The sources' [affected monitors](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/affected-monitors/) and [participants](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/participants/) are added to the target.
- **The highest severity wins.** The target takes the highest severity among itself and its sources.
- **Updates stay on the source.** The target lists each source with its own updates, so you can read the full history from the target.
- **Published sources point to the target.** A source that is on a status page gets one public update, "Merged into" followed by the target's title, and the target is added to that page.
- **Sources are quiet.** A merge sends no resolved notification and no subscriber email for the sources.
- **Paging carries over.** The sources' escalations stop. If a source was still paging and the target was not, the target's escalation starts.

## Merge in the dashboard

Open the incident you want to keep and click **Merge incidents into this one**. Pick the duplicates under **Open incidents to merge** and click **Merge incidents**. The button is off on a resolved incident: reopen it first.

## Merge through the API

The incident in the path is the target. Send between 1 and 20 source ids:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.siteqwality.com/incident/$TARGET_ID/merge \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "source_ids": ["<source-id-1>", "<source-id-2>"] }'
```

## What stops a merge

A merge is all or nothing. It fails, and changes nothing, when:

- a source is the target itself,
- a source is already resolved,
- the target is resolved (reopen it first),
- a source was already merged into another incident, or
- the target was itself merged into another incident (merge into that one instead).

The error codes are in the [incident reference](/incident-management/incidents/reference/#conflicts-409).
