An **incident** is the durable record of "something is broken." Every page, every escalation, every status-page update hangs off an incident. They're created two ways:

1. **Auto**: when a monitor linked to a [service](/incident-management/services/overview/) in `auto` mode starts failing, Site Qwality opens an incident with `auto_created: true`. A service has at most one open auto-created incident, and every other monitor of the service that fails joins it. Monitors with no service never open incidents. See [auto-created incidents](/incident-management/services/overview/#auto-created-incidents).
2. **Manual**: when you create one in the dashboard or via `POST /incident`. Use this for non-monitor events ("partial Stripe outage breaking webhooks," "a cron job took down the queue").

Either way the lifecycle is the same.

## Anatomy of an incident

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `title` | Short, scannable label. `"Elevated 5xx errors on /api/checkout"`. Show this in alerts. |
| `auto_created` | `true` when a monitor state change opened the incident; `false` for manual ones. |
| `severity` | `minor`, `major`, or `critical`. Drives display priority on status pages and dashboards. |
| `status` | `investigating` → `identified` → `monitoring` → `resolved`. Forward-only in practice; see notes. |
| `responder_status` | `triggered` (no one's looking), `acknowledged` (someone's on it), or `resolved`. |
| `acknowledged_at` / `acknowledged_by` | Set when a responder acknowledges. Stops escalation. |
| `monitors[]` | The monitors this incident is about. Each has an `impact`, a `source` (`auto` or `manual`) and `joined_at` / `recovered_at` times, and drives which status-page components show the incident. See [affected monitors and impact](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/affected-monitors/). |
| `affected_http_job_ids` | Deprecated: the HTTP monitors in `monitors[]`. Use `monitors`. |
| `participants[]` | The people working the incident, each with a role: `lead`, `responder` or `comms`. See [participants](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/participants/). |
| `merged_into_id` / `resolution_reason` | Set on an incident that was [merged](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/merge-incidents/) into another. It is resolved, with `resolution_reason` set to `merged`. Otherwise both are `null`. |
| `merged_from[]` | On the incident that others were merged into: each merged incident's `id`, `title`, `created_at` and its own `updates`. |
| `service_id` | Optionally tag an incident to a service (a logical grouping of monitors). You can [move it to another service](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/#move-an-incident-to-another-service). |
| `status_page_ids` | The status pages the incident is published on. |
| `public_title` | The title those pages show, so an internal title like `db-7 failover` never reaches customers. Empty shows `title`. |
| `updates[]` | Chronological list of `IncidentUpdate`s, each `public` (on status pages) or `internal` (a note for your team). |

## Status vs responder_status

Two parallel state machines that often confuse new users:

- **`status`** is the *external* / *customer-facing* progress: "we're still investigating," "we know the cause," "fix is rolled out, we're watching." It's what shows on the status page, and only public updates and resolving move it.
- **`responder_status`** is the *internal* / *responder* state: "no one's on this," "someone has it," "we're done." It's what drives escalation and ack tracking.

A status page incident can be `status: monitoring` while still `responder_status: acknowledged`, meaning the customer-facing message is "we're keeping an eye on it" but a human is still actively babysitting.

## Severity guidance

| Severity | Use for |
|---|---|
| `minor` | Single non-critical endpoint flapping; degraded performance below SLA but functional. |
| `major` | Customer-facing feature broken for a subset of users; one region down with traffic still served from another. |
| `critical` | Full outage; data loss; security incident. Wakes everyone up. |

Severity sets the urgency of the page: `critical` and `major` incidents page at **high** urgency, `minor` at **low**, and each responder's [notification rules](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/notification-rules/) decide how each urgency reaches them. It does not choose a different escalation policy: the policy comes from the incident's service.

Raising an incident's severity from `minor` to `major` or `critical` pages the current escalation level again. An acknowledged incident goes back to `triggered`, so an earlier acknowledgment does not silence the new page.

## Lifecycle: a typical auto-created incident

1. HTTP check `prod-api`, linked to the `API` service in `auto` mode, flips from `success` to `failure` at 03:14:22.
2. Incident opens automatically with `auto_created: true`, `status: investigating`, `responder_status: triggered`, and the service's default severity.
3. The service's escalation policy pages the on-call through their notification rules. Separately, the check's notification groups send their usual monitor alert.
4. The on-call engineer receives an SMS at 03:14:30. They open the dashboard and click **Acknowledge** at 03:15:10.
5. `responder_status` flips to `acknowledged`. Escalation stops. With no lead yet, the engineer becomes the incident's `lead`.
6. They post an incident update at 03:18: `status: identified`, message `"Database failover stuck. Manually promoting standby."`
7. The fix lands at 03:24. The check goes green on the next tick, and `prod-api` records its `recovered_at`.
8. Every monitor on the incident has recovered, but the incident was acknowledged and the service uses the default [auto-resolve](/incident-management/services/overview/#auto-resolve) setting, `unless_acknowledged`. It stays open for the engineer to resolve at 03:27: `status: resolved`, `responder_status: resolved`, `resolved_at` set.

Had nobody acknowledged it, the incident would have resolved itself at 03:24. If `prod-api` fails again within the service's reopen window after that, the same incident reopens instead of a new one opening.

You can override any of these transitions manually if needed: the auto-resolution will be skipped if you've already moved the incident to `resolved` yourself.

## When to create manual incidents

- A third-party service breaks something for your customers (Stripe, AWS, your CDN). The monitors don't know, but you do.
- A bug in production that doesn't manifest as a monitor failure (UI broken, auth flow broken for a subset of users).
- Coordination of a complex outage across multiple teams. One canonical incident with a clear timeline beats six Slack threads.

For these, use `POST /incident` (standalone) or `POST /status_page/{id}/incident` (creates it on that page and emails the page's confirmed subscribers). Add `service_id` to page the service's escalation policy.

## Acknowledgment

Acknowledging an incident is **not** resolving it. Ack stops the page (so escalation won't keep paging), but the incident stays open until someone explicitly resolves it. This is intentional: the responder claims ownership, then has the time to actually fix the thing without their phone going off.

The first person to acknowledge an incident that has no lead becomes its `lead` participant.

An incident that a monitor outage opened automatically is linked to that outage's own [acknowledgment](/incident-management/notifications/how-to/acknowledge-outages/#incidents-opened-by-the-outage). Acknowledging the outage acknowledges the incident if it is still `triggered`, and acknowledging the incident records the outage acknowledgment. Incidents you create by hand are never linked to an outage.

## Working an incident with others

- **Participants.** Record who is working the incident and in what role (`lead`, `responder` or `comms`), and page more people from a user, an on-call schedule or an escalation policy. See [participants](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/participants/).
- **Affected monitors.** An incident lists the monitors it is about, when each joined and recovered, and how badly each is affected. See [affected monitors and impact](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/affected-monitors/).
- **Merging.** Fold duplicate incidents into one. See [merge incidents](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/merge-incidents/).
- **Moving.** Change an incident's service when it was filed under the wrong one. See [move an incident to another service](/incident-management/incidents/how-to/manual-incidents/#move-an-incident-to-another-service).
