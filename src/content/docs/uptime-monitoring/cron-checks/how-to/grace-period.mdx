If your nightly backup pinged at 02:00 yesterday and pings at 02:05 today, is it late? With no grace period it is: the check expects a ping within `check_interval_seconds` of the last one. A grace period gives the job extra time before an alert.

## How lateness is decided

A heartbeat is due one interval plus its grace after the newest of these:

- its last finished-run ping (a `/start` does not count),
- when you created it,
- when you last resumed it.

```
due = newest of (last finished ping, created, resumed) + check_interval_seconds + grace_seconds
```

Site Qwality checks every heartbeat against its due time once a minute, so a missed run alerts within about a minute of it. A notification group's [send delay](/incident-management/notifications/default-routing/#the-group-delay) adds to that.

A ping that arrives late but inside the grace counts for the run it was late for, and the next due time is measured from that ping. A late run never eats into the next one.

For example, a daily job (`check_interval_seconds: 86400`) with `grace_seconds: 600` last pinged at 02:00 on Monday:

| What happens | Result |
|---|---|
| No ping by 02:10 on Tuesday | The check goes down at about 02:11 and alerts. |
| A ping at 02:07 on Tuesday | No alert. The next run is due by 02:17 on Wednesday. |

## Set the grace

In the dashboard, set **Grace Period Minutes** when you add or edit the check (0 to 1440 minutes). Through the API, `grace_seconds` is 0 to 86400 seconds (one day) and defaults to `0`. Send it when you create the check with `POST /cron/job`, or change it with `PUT /cron/job/{cron_job_id}`. Every field is in the [cron check reference](/uptime-monitoring/cron-checks/reference/).

Changing the interval or grace applies from the last ping at once: the check goes down right away if its last ping is already outside the window, and comes back up if its last ping was a success that fits it.

## How to tune

### Job whose finish time varies

If your job sometimes takes 30 minutes and sometimes 6 hours:

- Set `check_interval_seconds` to the **schedule between runs**, not the runtime. A 6-hour job that runs once a day still has a 24-hour interval.
- Ping at the **end** of the run, not the start.
- Set `grace_seconds` to the spread in finish times. A backup that finishes between 02:05 and 02:40 needs a grace of at least 35 minutes (`2100`).

### Job with a tight schedule

If your job *must* finish within 5 minutes of its usual time:

- Set `grace_seconds` to `300` or less, so a run that is later than that alerts.
- Don't lower `check_interval_seconds` below the actual interval, since that alerts on healthy runs.
- To limit how long a run takes, emit a **runtime metric** and alert when it crosses your threshold. The cron check stays as the catch-all for "didn't run at all."

### Job that runs every minute

With no grace, one lost ping on a job that runs every minute alerts. Consider:

- A grace of a couple of runs (`120` for a job every minute), so a single lost ping passes while a job that stops still alerts within a few minutes.
- Retries on the ping, so a single network blip doesn't trigger the check (see [send heartbeats reliably](/uptime-monitoring/cron-checks/how-to/heartbeats/)).

## What about jobs that fail mid-run

A job that knows it failed can say so: ping `/fail` or send a non-zero exit code and the check goes down right away, whatever the grace ([how](/uptime-monitoring/cron-checks/how-to/heartbeats/)). If the job crashes after starting but before pinging, the check goes down when the run is due, like any missed run. The next successful ping brings it back right away.

For a failure rate rather than a single run, have the job emit a metric, like `job.runs.completed{name="nightly_backup", status="success"}`, and alert on it.
