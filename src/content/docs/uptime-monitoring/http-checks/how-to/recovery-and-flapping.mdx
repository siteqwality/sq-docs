A site that comes back for one check and fails again sends you an all clear and then a second outage alert a minute later. A site that bounces up and down every few minutes sends a pair of alerts every time. Two HTTP check settings deal with this:

- `recovery_period_runs` waits for several healthy checks before the all clear.
- Flap hold holds short outages while a monitor flaps and sends one notice instead. It is an account setting that each monitor can override.

| Field | Type | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recovery_period_runs` | int | `2` | `1` to `10`. `1` sends the all clear on the first healthy check. |
| `flap_hold_override` | bool \| null | `null` | `null` follows the account setting, `true` always holds, `false` never holds. |

In the monitor form they are **Recovery Period** and **Hold alerts while flapping**, under **Alerting**. Through the API, with an [API key](/getting-started/account-and-team/#generate-api-keys) exported as `SITEQWALITY_API_KEY` and `$JOB_ID` set to the check's `id`:

```bash
curl -X PUT https://api.siteqwality.com/http/job/$JOB_ID \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $SITEQWALITY_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "recovery_period_runs": 2, "flap_hold_override": null }'
```

Send the check's current `auth_type`, `content_type` and `custom_http_headers` in the same `PUT` if it uses them: leaving them out clears them.

## Recovery period

The monitor goes back to `success` on the first healthy check. Uptime, SLA reports and the outage's recorded duration all use that first healthy check. What waits for the recovery period:

- the all clear notification on every channel;
- automatic resolution of the incident, if the outage opened one through a [service](/incident-management/services/overview/).

A failed check inside the period resets the count. If the monitor goes back down before the all clear was due, nobody is told twice: any open incident stays open, and the outage alert that already went out still stands. When the all clear does go out, it gives the time of the last recovery.

A 60 second monitor with `recovery_period_runs: 2` sends the all clear one minute after the first healthy check.

## Flap hold

A monitor is flapping after **4 status changes within 30 minutes**. While it flaps:

- One **Flapping** notice goes out to the monitor's email, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Discord, Google Chat, Mattermost, Telegram and Pushbullet channels.
- Each new outage is held. If the monitor recovers within **5 minutes**, the held outage and its recovery are dropped together. If it is still down after 5 minutes, the held outage is sent to every channel, paging ones included, and a linked service opens its incident.
- Once the monitor has had no status change for **15 minutes**, a **Stable** summary says how many outages were held.

SMS, PagerDuty, Opsgenie and Pushover never get the Flapping or Stable notices, only outages that broke through. Webhooks get no flap notices.

Held outages show on the monitor page with **Alert held while flapping**, and **then sent** once one broke through.

### Turn flap hold on or off

Whether an HTTP monitor holds depends on two things:

- The account setting `flap_hold`, under **Notifications**, **Settings**. It is on by default.
- The monitor's own `flap_hold_override`. In the monitor form, **Hold alerts while flapping** lets you follow the Notifications setting, always hold or never hold.

An override wins over the account setting. A monitor's read-only `flap_hold` is the result: the override if it has one, otherwise the account setting, otherwise on. `PUT /http/job/{job_id}` takes `flap_hold_override` (leave it out to keep it, send `null` to follow the account), and a request that sends `flap_hold` sets the override.

## Heartbeat and browser monitors

Heartbeat (cron) monitors and browser checks hold flaps the same way: the same 4 changes in 30 minutes, the same 5-minute break-through, the same **Flapping** notice and **Stable** summary, and no incident or escalation while an outage is held. Two differences:

- They follow the account's `flap_hold` setting only, with no per-monitor override.
- They have no recovery period. The all clear goes out on the first healthy check.

Each held outage appears in the monitor's activity feed as **Outage held**, followed by **Held outage sent** if it broke through. A heartbeat checks on its own interval, so a held outage is decided by the one-minute tick, not by the next ping.
